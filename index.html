    AI/ML Interview Map¶

    One storyline from linear regression to agent harnesses. Every topic answers the same questions: what problem it solved, how it works, where it breaks, what replaced it, and what interviewers ask about it.

    Foundations & production Act I · learn from features Act II · deep learning Act III · attention & scale Act IV · align, ground, act

    Why this site exists¶

    Interview preparation usually breaks down the same way. You start with Transformers, realise you need RNNs, go back to LSTMs, then jump forward to RAG and MCP, and the picture never comes together.

    The fix is to learn AI/ML as one story. Every technique exists because an earlier one failed at something. If you know each method's weakness, you know why the next one was invented, and the order explains itself. That chain of weaknesses is also exactly what interviewers probe: "Why not an RNN?", "Why divide by √d?", "Why RAG instead of fine-tuning?"

    The storyline in four acts¶

    1. The toolkitFoundationsMath, ML fundamentals and metrics that every later topic reuses.
    2. 1950s–2000sAct I · Learn from featuresPeople design the features; models learn weights on top of them.
    3. 2006–2016Act II · Learn the featuresBecause hand-made features capped accuracy.
    4. 2017–2022Act III · Attention and scaleBecause RNNs were slow and forgot.
    5. 2022–nowAct IV · Align, ground, actBecause LLMs only continue text.
    6. ThroughoutProductionMLOps and system design: making any of it work for real users.

    How every topic is written¶

    Each topic answers the same seven questions, in the order you should use when answering in an interview:

    1. Problem before it: what existed, and why it wasn't good enough.
    2. Core idea: one sentence of intuition.
    3. How it works: the mechanism, then the math interviewers expect.
    4. Strengths.
    5. Weaknesses: where it breaks, which is usually the follow-up question.
    6. What came next, and why: the bridge to the next topic.
    7. Interview questions: with answers hidden until you click, so you can test yourself first.

    Your progress stays in your browser

    Every study page has a status control under its title: Not started, Learning, Confident. The My Progress page totals them, and the Flashcard Drill remembers which questions you knew. Nothing leaves your browser; export it from My Progress to move it to another device.
